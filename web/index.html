Blood sugar ranges used to define prediabetes

A risk score of 5 or higher (out of 10) means you are at high risk[1]. Only a blood test can show whether your blood sugar is in the prediabetes range[5]:

Blood test ranges for normal, prediabetes and diabetes
TestNormalPrediabetesDiabetes
Fasting plasma glucoseno food for at least 8 hours below 100 mg/dLbelow 5.6 mmol/L 100–125 mg/dL5.6–6.9 mmol/L 126 mg/dL or higher7.0 mmol/L or higher
A1Caverage over about 3 months below 5.7%below 39 mmol/mol 5.7–6.4%39–47 mmol/mol 6.5% or higher48 mmol/mol or higher
2-hour glucose tolerance test75 g oral glucose load below 140 mg/dLbelow 7.8 mmol/L 140–199 mg/dL7.8–11.0 mmol/L 200 mg/dL or higher11.1 mmol/L or higher

Ranges follow the American Diabetes Association[5]. Some organisations, including the WHO, set the lower limit of impaired fasting glucose at 110 mg/dL (6.1 mmol/L). A1C can be unreliable in anaemia or haemoglobin variants. Abnormal results should be confirmed by repeat testing and interpreted by a doctor.

Prediabetes can often be reversed

About 98 million U.S. adults, more than 1 in 3, have prediabetes, and more than 80% of them do not know it[1]. In the Diabetes Prevention Program, intensive lifestyle change (a goal of about 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of brisk activity a week) reduced the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by 58%[3][4].

Score of 5 or higher? Ask your doctor about a blood test, and about a recognised lifestyle change programme[2].
